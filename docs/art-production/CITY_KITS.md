## Purpose

Define approved city-specific identity pillars and production parameters beneath
the frozen Art Bible, frozen Prompt Bible, City-skin Framework, and Technical
Asset Contract.

City kits translate researched place identity into restrained, modular secondary
qualities. They do not redefine global gameplay meaning, category recognition,
accessibility, technical compatibility, or the shared Masala Run art style.

## Authority & Inheritance

```text
Frozen Art Bible
→ Frozen Prompt Bible
→ Technical Asset Contract
→ City Kit
→ Category-specific Asset Brief
→ Generated Asset
→ In-game Review
```

Every city-skinned asset inherits its category module before this city kit.
When city identity conflicts with gameplay clarity, accessibility, modularity,
procedural reuse, or technical support, weaken or remove the city treatment.

## City-Kit Production Requirements

Each production prompt using a city kit must declare:

* active city and city-kit version
* approved identity pillars and applicable categories
* permitted, overused, and prohibited cues
* foreign-city exclusions
* protected gameplay characteristics
* permitted treatment strength
* day/night state and optional atmosphere variant
* accessibility requirements
* relevant category module and Technical Asset Contract version
* naming, metadata, output, and in-game review requirements

Shared asset families are preferred. City-specific material, construction,
surface, palette, and minor detail layers remain separable where practical.

## Mumbai City Kit

### Frozen Identity Direction

Mumbai represents everyday neighbourhood streets: practical, lived-in,
commercial, layered, and readable. It is not tourist, cinematic, monumental,
or postcard Mumbai.

The four approved identity pillars are:

1. Dense Layered Street Commerce — primary
2. Monsoon-Worn Urban Surfaces — primary
3. Mixed-Age Practical Architecture — primary
4. Transit and Street-Mobility Echoes — supporting-strength

No additional Mumbai identity pillar may be introduced without formal city-kit
review and documented justification.

### Pillar 1 — Dense Layered Street Commerce

**Pillar strength:** Primary.

**Identity statement:** Everyday commerce creates a layered but organised edge
rhythm through compact shopfronts, temporary attachments, serving elements, and
stored goods. It supports neighbourhood activity without becoming a market
theme park or continuous wall of clutter.

**Supporting materials:** Painted plaster, concrete, corrugated metal,
tarpaulin, wood, restrained metal fixtures, simple containers, and cloth.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Compact rectangular shopfront
masses; horizontal shutter rhythms; shallow awnings and overhangs; controlled
tarpaulin extensions; stacked low-to-medium prop groups; clear primary anchors;
and deliberate breathing gaps between clusters.

**Relevant prop families:** Shutters, awnings, tarpaulin extensions, small food
and tea counters, stacked crates and containers, handcarts, stools, vessels,
serving surfaces, compact stall components, and temporary commercial
attachments.

Food culture, including chai and vada-pav-style street commerce, belongs inside
this pillar. It must remain one restrained part of ordinary street commerce and
must not become a separate food-festival or theme-park pillar.

**Surface treatments:** Faded paint, mild handling wear, restrained repair
marks, quiet container variation, and clean simplified material cues. Avoid
dense grime, tiny merchandise detail, or readable packaging.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Warm natural daylight on shallow commercial
layers; at night, compact warm shop-light sources at the edges with controlled
spill. Keep the road centre calm.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Warm weathered neutrals with faded teal or
blue, muted green, restrained red, and controlled yellow accents. Accents remain
secondary to gameplay colours.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Shutter slat rhythm, shallow awning bands,
rectangular signboard blocks, stacked-container rhythm, and simple tarpaulin fold
geometry. Signboards remain abstract shapes with no readable text.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No endless bazaar, chaotic merchandise
wall, food-theme spectacle, readable menus or brands, vendor caricatures,
poverty theatre, uncontrolled crowds, or city identity carried by one food item.

**Applicable asset categories:** Environmental props, modular micro-clusters,
frontages, architectural attachments, selected safe edge overlays, and
practical environmental lighting.

**Treatment strength:** Strong for environment and practical lights; absent from
unrelated gameplay categories unless another category rule explicitly permits a
restrained accent.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve the same shopfront, attachment, and cluster
structure. Night may reveal restrained practical sources; it must not redesign
the cluster, add neon spectacle, or increase density.

**Monsoon-variant implications:** Optional rain-protection tarps, mild dampness,
and restrained material darkening may be added in an explicit monsoon variant.
Default commerce assets are not permanently rainy, flooded, or wet.

### Pillar 2 — Monsoon-Worn Urban Surfaces

**Pillar strength:** Primary.

**Identity statement:** Climate response and repeated repair give ordinary
urban surfaces a subtly weathered, maintained, and humidity-aware character.
Default Mumbai remains readable and cared-for rather than uniformly dirty or
permanently rain-soaked.

**Supporting materials:** Weathered painted plaster, repaired concrete, patched
asphalt, corrugated metal, tarpaulin, restrained drainage fixtures, pipes, and
simple rain-protection components.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Broad readable material fields;
small repair seams; compact drainage and pipe forms; shallow covers; restrained
surface breaks; and no silhouette noise created solely by weathering.

**Relevant prop families:** Tarpaulins, drain covers, downpipes, shallow gutters,
rain guards, repaired containers, maintenance barriers, utility fixtures, and
weather-protection attachments.

**Surface treatments:** Faded paint, repaired concrete, patched asphalt, mild
staining, dampened edge surfaces, restrained humidity response, and occasional
surface ageing. Normal assets use these cues subtly.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Daylight retains restrained surface contrast.
Night may use limited cool ambient separation against warm practical lights.
Humidity or rain-light response belongs only to an approved monsoon variant.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Warm concrete grey, dusty beige, softened
blue-green ageing, quiet asphalt variation, and controlled darker damp accents.
No surface state is communicated through colour alone.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Patch geometry, repair seams, shallow drainage
rhythm, simplified water-shedding angles, and restrained vertical weathering
marks.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No permanent rain, flooding, wet-road
default, sewage imagery, extreme grime, every surface appearing dirty,
photorealistic decay, dramatic reflections, or puddles used as passive
decoration when they resemble gameplay hazards.

**Applicable asset categories:** Frontages, road materials, safe road overlays,
environmental props, micro-clusters, practical lighting response, and optional
atmospheric overlays.

**Treatment strength:** Strong on environmental surfaces; moderate for approved
atmosphere; minimal or absent on functional gameplay assets and UI.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve material geometry and repair logic. Night
may adjust local contrast and edge support but cannot add permanent gloss,
bloom, larger marks, or harder readability.

**Monsoon-variant implications:** Stronger dampness, subtle surface darkening,
limited mist, restrained rain-light response, and separately approved puddles
are permitted only in an explicit monsoon variant. Gameplay puddles remain
separate hazards; passive puddle-like overlays require approved safe-overlay
category separation.

### Pillar 3 — Mixed-Age Practical Architecture

**Pillar strength:** Primary.

**Identity statement:** Ordinary streets combine older plaster and masonry with
newer concrete construction, repairs, additions, and visible practical services.
The result is irregular but functional, not a heritage showcase.

**Supporting materials:** Painted plaster, masonry, concrete, corrugated metal,
metal shutters and grilles, wood, tarpaulin, restrained pipes, and exterior
fixtures. Modern glass and steel may appear occasionally but do not define the
pillar. Historic stone and decorative heritage materials remain secondary and
require justification.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Layered rectangular masses;
compact balconies; shallow overhangs; grilled openings; irregular but readable
frontage bays; repaired edges; exterior pipes; and global rounded-form softening
through awnings, containers, and selected corners.

**Relevant prop families:** Shutters, grilles, pipes, meters, compact balconies,
overhangs, exterior fixtures, awnings, repair panels, utility boxes, and modular
frontage attachments.

**Surface treatments:** Weathered plaster beside cleaner repairs, simplified
masonry cues, patched concrete, restrained paint variation, and quiet age
contrast. Surface ageing remains moderate and mobile-readable.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Warm daylight clarifies broad planes and
shallow depth. Night uses compact practical fixtures and controlled edge
separation without turning architecture into a luminous backdrop.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Warm concrete grey, dusty beige, faded teal
or blue, muted green, restrained red, and small controlled yellow accents.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Mixed bay widths, grille rhythm, shallow balcony
bands, pipe runs, repair rectangles, overhang layers, and restrained construction
seams.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No city identity based only on colonial or
heritage architecture, no literal monument facade, no palace-like frontage, no
deep cinematic street, no slum caricature, and no hyper-detailed decay.

**Applicable asset categories:** Frontages, architectural elements,
environmental props, modular micro-clusters, practical lighting sources, and
restrained background edge rhythm.

**Treatment strength:** Strong for frontages and architecture; moderate or lower
for attached environment; never a reason to redesign gameplay objects.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve structure, openings, depth, crop, and
attachments. Night changes only controlled contrast and practical-light
visibility.

**Monsoon-variant implications:** Optional rain guards, slightly stronger
weathering response, restrained damp edges, and approved drainage emphasis may
appear without changing the default architectural family.

### Pillar 4 — Transit and Street-Mobility Echoes

**Pillar strength:** Supporting-strength. This pillar supports the three primary
pillars and must not dominate Mumbai identity.

**Identity statement:** Restrained transport and movement-related forms suggest
an active neighbourhood network through modular edge assets and infrastructure,
not through traffic spectacle or complete vehicle scenes.

**Supporting materials:** Painted metal, restrained black-and-yellow accents,
wood, asphalt, concrete barriers, corrugated panels, simple glass cues where
technically safe, and practical street fixtures.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Compact vehicle-form echoes;
rounded rectangular masses; simple canopy and wheel relationships; low roadside
barriers; vertical stop-like structures; and clear isolated silhouettes that
remain outside the playable road centre.

**Relevant prop families:** Auto-rickshaw or taxi form-language variants,
delivery carts, handcarts, roadside barriers, bus-stop-like structural forms,
restrained road markings, mobility-related street infrastructure, and modular
parked edge vehicles.

Vehicles remain modular edge assets. They must not dominate the environment,
form complete traffic scenes, obstruct gameplay readability, or imply moving
collision behaviour when passive.

**Surface treatments:** Restrained paint wear, simplified panel seams, mild
roadside repair, controlled barrier marks, and clean flat-cartoon material cues.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Day treatment remains quiet and world-native.
Night may add small approved practical markers or restrained reflections as
separate lighting treatment, never vehicle-wide glow or cinematic traffic light.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Controlled black-and-yellow transport
accents supported by warm neutrals, faded teal or blue, muted green, and
restrained red. Do not repeat black-and-yellow striping across unrelated assets.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Canopy arcs, compact wheel spacing, rounded panel
geometry, barrier rhythm, stop-like vertical proportions, and short restrained
marking segments.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No traffic or honking comedy, vehicle
crowds, literal local-train scenes, transport icon pasted on unrelated assets,
excessive black-and-yellow stripes, moving-vehicle confusion, or objective-like
bus-stop beacons.

**Applicable asset categories:** Environmental props, modular micro-clusters,
selected architectural attachments, safe road markings or overlays, and
restrained practical lighting.

**Treatment strength:** Supporting-strength and generally strong only on its own
approved environmental families; minimal or absent elsewhere.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve the same asset geometry, passive role, and
screen-edge placement. Night changes only controlled local contrast and
separately managed practical lights.

**Monsoon-variant implications:** Optional rain covers, subtle damp material
response, and restrained tyre-area darkening may appear in explicit variants.
No default flooding, traffic spray, or permanent rainfall.

### Environmental Density

**Medium overall density with denser modular micro-clusters and deliberate breathing gaps.**

* Protect the playable road centre.
* Place visual density primarily at the left and right edges.
* Use layered clusters instead of continuous clutter.
* Preserve clear gaps between clusters and frontage rhythms.
* Avoid uncontrolled crowding, edge walls, and screen-wide commerce scenes.
* Preserve mobile gameplay readability and procedural placement compatibility.

### Shape Language

**Layered rectangular structures softened by awnings, tarps, rounded containers and the global Masala Run rounded-form grammar.**

Preserve clean flat-cartoon style, rounded forms with selective sharp accents,
flat fills with at most one darker shade, moderate detail, readable silhouettes,
high bird’s-eye camera compatibility, and mobile-scale readability. Avoid harsh
industrial geometry, hyper-angular treatment, or city-specific rendering that
looks like a separate game.

### Material & Palette Direction

Primary material family:

* painted plaster
* concrete
* corrugated metal
* tarpaulin
* wood
* asphalt
* restrained metal fixtures

Modern glass and steel are occasional secondary materials. Historic stone and
decorative heritage material require pillar-based justification.

Mumbai’s environmental palette direction is:

**Warm weathered neutrals with restrained urban accent colours.**

The production palette family may use warm concrete grey, dusty beige, faded
teal or blue, muted green, restrained red, controlled yellow, and controlled
black-and-yellow transport accents. Exact colour values remain asset-brief
parameters reviewed against the global gameplay colour system; this kit does
not freeze unsupported hexadecimal values.

Reject saturated Bollywood treatment, full industrial greyscale, colour-only
city identity, or any change to global ownership, danger, rarity, health,
warning, or interaction colours.

### Day, Night & Optional Monsoon Treatment

Day and night use the same structural Mumbai city skin.

**Day:** Warm natural urban daylight, restrained surface contrast, a readable
road centre, and weathered materials without excessive grime.

**Night:** Warm practical shop lights, restrained bulbs or other approved
fixtures, subtle cool ambient contrast, controlled edge lighting, and a calm
readable road centre.

Reject neon-heavy cinematic Mumbai, uncontrolled bloom, large permanent glow
pools, road-wide lighting noise, larger night variants, or reduced night
readability.

**Optional monsoon variant:** Humidity, subtle dampness, restrained rain-light
response, limited mist, and controlled surface darkening. Puddles appear only as
separately approved gameplay hazards or safe road-overlay assets with explicit
category separation. Permanent rain, flooding, and wet roads are not part of
the default Mumbai state.

### Landmark & Iconic-reference Rules

Use ordinary street identity and landmark echoes, never literal tourist
landmarks. An approved echo may borrow only abstract proportion, material,
structural rhythm, construction logic, silhouette tendency, or restrained
geometry.

Do not create Gateway of India miniatures, Marine Drive replicas, literal local-
train scenes, skyline postcards, monument-shaped props, tourist-poster
compositions, or objective-like landmark beacons.

### Mumbai-specific Prohibitions

Reject:

* Bollywood posters, celebrity imagery, and film-industry clichés
* slum caricatures or poverty used as visual identity
* uncontrolled crowd chaos or traffic and honking comedy
* every surface appearing dirty, excessive grime, or photorealistic decay
* decorative religious or political symbols
* literal landmark miniatures and local-train imagery used everywhere
* permanent monsoon flooding, permanent rain, or default wet roads
* excessive black-and-yellow striping
* readable Hindi, Marathi, or English text
* logos, trademarks, brand marks, and recognisable packaging
* tourist-poster, postcard, cinematic, or neon-heavy Mumbai
* mixed-city contamination
* unrelated Jaisalmer, Rajasthan, or foreign-city materials, architecture,
  motifs, palettes, religious/cultural symbols, and environmental cues

Abstract signboard blocks may establish commercial rhythm, but must contain no
readable letters, words, numbers, logos, or pseudo-text.

### Asset-category Treatment Strength

#### Strong Treatment

* environmental props
* modular micro-clusters
* frontages and architectural elements
* road materials and safe road overlays
* practical environmental lighting

#### Moderate Treatment

* approved hazards
* selected enemies and selected pickups
* restrained ambient VFX
* atmospheric overlays

#### Restrained Treatment

* player
* attacks and projectiles
* functional VFX
* World-space UI and HUD

#### Minimal or No Treatment

* critical telegraphs
* danger boundaries and ownership grammar
* accessibility-essential UI
* globally fixed gameplay symbols

Mumbai treatment must never change gameplay meaning, category recognition,
primary silhouette, behaviour, footprint, collision or hitbox perception,
scale, ownership, danger, rarity, direction, timing, state, movement language,
anchors, icon meaning, or accessibility-essential cues.

### Shared & Mumbai-exclusive Assets

Prefer shared asset families with Mumbai-specific material and detail layers.
A Mumbai-exclusive asset is allowed only when it:

* supports a documented Mumbai identity pillar
* cannot be represented credibly through a shared asset family
* remains modular and procedurally reusable
* follows the global shape and style grammar
* preserves its category and protected gameplay characteristics
* does not create a complete scene or baked background
* includes explicit written justification in its asset brief

Novelty, beauty, charm, or city recognition alone is not sufficient.

### Technical Production Rules

Every Mumbai asset brief inherits the relevant Prompt Bible category module,
City-skin Framework, Technical Asset Contract, naming and metadata conventions,
accessibility requirements, and in-game approval gate.

City treatment cannot change contract-governed dimensions, alpha behaviour,
transparent-background requirements, padding, pivots, anchors, scale,
perspective, orientation, frame consistency, slicing, naming, metadata, export,
safe-area behaviour, or supported runtime transformations unless an explicitly
approved technical variant requires it.

Assets remain isolated, modular, palette-compatible, and transparent-background
where their category requires it. Do not create complete streets, backgrounds,
gameplay scenes, painted day/night pairs, flattened UI, or unsupported animation
and rendering behaviour.

### Mumbai Acceptance Criteria

Accept a Mumbai city-skinned output only when it:

1. Clearly derives from at least one applicable approved Mumbai pillar.
2. Reads as everyday neighbourhood Mumbai through multiple restrained cues.
3. Preserves the global Masala Run shape, style, camera, and gameplay hierarchy.
4. Preserves category, silhouette, footprint, ownership, direction, timing, and state.
5. Preserves accessibility and gameplay meaning without colour-only recognition.
6. Uses category-appropriate treatment strength.
7. Keeps medium overall density, denser edge clusters, and deliberate breathing gaps.
8. Protects the road centre and remains readable at mobile gameplay scale.
9. Preserves the same structural asset across day and night.
10. Treats monsoon response as optional rather than the default.
11. Avoids literal landmarks, stereotypes, readable text, and mixed-city cues.
12. Remains modular, procedurally reusable, and technically contract-compliant.
13. Remains valid when optional Mumbai accents are reduced or removed.
14. Passes in-game review at target scale beside the courier.

### Frozen Mumbai Identity Summary

1. Mumbai means everyday neighbourhood streets, not tourist or cinematic imagery.
2. Its four pillars are Dense Layered Street Commerce, Monsoon-Worn Urban
   Surfaces, Mixed-Age Practical Architecture, and supporting-strength Transit
   and Street-Mobility Echoes.
3. Density is medium overall, with denser edge micro-clusters and breathing gaps.
4. Layered rectangular rhythm is softened by global rounded forms.
5. Materials and palette use warm weathered neutrals with restrained urban accents.
6. Night uses restrained warm shop-light character and a calm road centre.
7. Monsoon response is an optional controlled variant, never the default state.
8. Landmark echoes replace literal landmarks.
9. Strong stereotype, readable-text, and mixed-city prevention is mandatory.
10. Gameplay meaning and accessibility remain protected in every treatment.

This Mumbai identity is frozen. Changes require formal city-kit review,
documented justification, version/status updates, and impact review against the
Art Bible, Prompt Bible, Technical Asset Contract, and dependent asset briefs.

## Jaisalmer City Kit

### Frozen Identity Direction

Jaisalmer represents **everyday inhabited desert-city Jaisalmer**: practical,
warm, lived-in, and recognisable through sandstone construction,
desert-climate adaptation, compact commerce, and restrained craft traditions.

It is not tourist-postcard imagery, a literal-monument collection, cinematic
desert fantasy, generic Rajasthan, royal or palace spectacle, endless dunes,
constant sandstorms, camel-led identity, or decorative overload.

The four approved Jaisalmer identity pillars are:

1. Layered Sandstone Architecture — primary-strength
2. Desert-Climate Adaptation — primary-strength
3. Compact Desert Commerce and Courtyard Life — primary-strength
4. Restrained Craft and Material Detail — supporting-strength

No additional Jaisalmer identity pillar may be introduced without formal
city-kit review and documented justification.

### Jaisalmer Pillar 1 — Layered Sandstone Architecture

**Pillar strength:** Primary-strength.

**Identity statement:** Inhabited sandstone construction creates Jaisalmer's
primary environmental identity through broad layered masses, shallow depth,
practical openings, repair, and weather-softened material variation rather than
monumental spectacle.

**Supporting materials:** Warm sandstone masonry, weathered plaster, wood,
restrained metal fixtures, occasional concrete repair, muted fabric shade, and
small clay or brass accents where structurally natural.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Layered rectangular building
masses, stepped platforms, parapets, shallow projecting balconies, simplified
jharokha-inspired openings, restrained arch-like openings, recessed doors,
stone platforms and steps, cropped frontage segments, and broad masses that
dominate over fine ornament.

Architecture remains modular, shallow, procedurally usable, confined to
non-playable edge zones, and readable at mobile gameplay scale. Fragile
ornamental silhouettes and realistic architectural illustration are prohibited.

**Relevant prop families:** Stone steps and platforms, recessed door modules,
parapet pieces, shallow balcony elements, simplified opening frames, restrained
stone seats, repair blocks, compact fixtures, and modular frontage attachments.

**Surface treatments:** Weather-softened masonry, repaired or varied stone,
restrained carved edges, simplified joints, sun-faded plaster, and controlled
age variation. Avoid dense carving, realistic erosion, or uniform gold material.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Warm clear daylight with controlled high-sun
character and readable contact shadows; at night, restrained lantern or shop
light near openings with subtle cool ambient separation.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Warm sandstone, muted ochre, dusty beige,
sun-faded cream, clay brown, and rare restrained indigo, maroon, teal, green, or
brass accents.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Stepped massing, parapet rhythm, shallow projection
patterns, simplified jharokha-inspired geometry, restrained arch rhythm,
lattice-inspired openings, broad stone borders, and repair-block variation.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No Jaisalmer Fort replica, fort-shaped
frontage, palace or haveli reproduction, heritage-hotel scene, postcard skyline,
carving-heavy facade, royal spectacle, or generic Rajasthan monument collage.

**Applicable asset categories:** Frontages, architectural elements,
environmental props, modular micro-clusters, road-edge structures, and practical
environmental lighting.

**Treatment strength:** Strong for frontages and architecture; strong or
moderate for related environmental assets; absent where sandstone treatment
would compromise gameplay category recognition.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve massing, openings, crop, footprint, and
attachment logic. Night changes controlled local contrast and practical-light
visibility only; it does not floodlight the architecture or make it larger.

**Optional atmosphere implications:** Mild dry-air haze, dusk warmth, or
restrained edge dust may support the architecture as separate variants without
obscuring structure or gameplay.

### Jaisalmer Pillar 2 — Desert-Climate Adaptation

**Pillar strength:** Primary-strength.

**Identity statement:** Ordinary buildings, commerce, objects, and surfaces
respond practically to heat, dryness, wind, and dust through shade, storage,
weathering, and restrained edge accumulation. Desert climate is contextual, not
a permanent visibility effect.

**Supporting materials:** Sun-faded sandstone and plaster, cloth, muted fabric,
wood, clay, brass, rope, woven material, restrained metal fixtures, and dry
compacted road material or asphalt where required.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Cloth shade covers, awnings,
canopies, shallow wind screens, water-storage vessel groups, shaded commercial
attachments, softened fabric shapes, broad dry surfaces, and edge-biased dust
forms with stable readable boundaries.

**Relevant prop families:** Water-storage vessels, shade panels, awnings,
canopies, tied cloth, storage jars, low wind screens, rope attachments, dry
maintenance objects, and restrained edge-sand overlay components.

**Surface treatments:** Sun fading, dry material wear, occasional wind-worn
marks, mild dust, limited edge sand accumulation, and quiet repair. The playable
road is never permanently covered by sand.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Warm clear daylight, restrained high-sun
character, protected road visibility, and subtle atmospheric dryness. Optional
variants may add mild windblown dust, heat haze, dry-air haze, softened distant
atmosphere, dusk, or limited edge accumulation.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Warm sandstone neutrals, dusty beige,
sun-faded cream, clay brown, muted fabric, and scarce cool or jewel accents for
separation. Climate recognition never relies on colour or brightness alone.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Shade-band geometry, tied-cloth rhythm, vessel
grouping, shallow wind marks, edge-biased accumulation, water-storage
silhouettes, and simplified sun-faded surface blocks.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No constant sandstorm, endless dunes,
permanent heat haze, road-wide sand cover, desert tents everywhere, visibility
loss, cinematic dust wall, decorative sand hazard, or continuous visual noise.

**Applicable asset categories:** Environmental props, modular micro-clusters,
frontages, safe road overlays, restrained atmospheric overlays, and practical
environmental lighting.

**Treatment strength:** Strong for environment and climate-response assets;
moderate for approved atmosphere; minimal or absent on functional gameplay
assets and interface information.

**Day/night implications:** Day may show controlled dryness and shade response.
Night preserves all geometry and surface meaning, using only restrained local
contrast and practical-light separation.

**Optional atmosphere implications:** Dry wind, mild dust, subtle heat haze,
dusk, softened distance, and limited sand accumulation are optional variants,
never the default state. They must not obscure the player, enemies, attacks,
projectiles, hazards, pickups, road boundaries, or World-space UI.

Sand hazards remain separate gameplay assets. Passive sand or dust overlays must
remain clearly non-actionable and inherit safe-overlay category rules.

### Jaisalmer Pillar 3 — Compact Desert Commerce and Courtyard Life

**Pillar strength:** Primary-strength.

**Identity statement:** Compact commerce and object arrangements suggest
inhabited neighbourhood and courtyard activity through practical edge clusters,
not complete markets, NPC crowds, or tourist souvenir displays.

**Supporting materials:** Wood, clay, brass, muted fabric, sandstone, baskets,
rope, woven material, restrained painted metal, storage jars, and occasional
glass cues where technically safe.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Compact shop openings, low
counters, wooden carts, rounded vessel groups, fabric shade panels, low seating,
benches, stacked baskets and jars, controlled asymmetry, one clear cluster
anchor, and larger breathing gaps than Mumbai.

**Relevant prop families:** Food and tea counters, sweets or snack carts, brass
and clay vessels, stacked baskets, fabric shade panels, wooden carts, storage
jars, low seating, benches, practical merchant attachments, and compact
shop-opening components.

**Surface treatments:** Dry handling wear, simplified vessel material cues,
sun-faded cloth, restrained wood marks, quiet brass highlights, and clean
storage variation. Avoid tiny merchandise detail and readable packaging.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Warm daylight with shaded pockets beneath
awnings and canopies; at night, limited warm shop or lantern light with a quiet
road centre. Optional dusk may add restrained warmth without cinematic grading.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Sandstone neutrals supported by clay brown,
muted fabric, restrained indigo and maroon, limited teal or green, and controlled
brass accents.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Vessel grouping, low-seat rhythm, cart-frame
geometry, broad textile bands, compact opening sequences, simple basket weave,
and courtyard-like spacing implied through object relationships rather than
baked ground or walls.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No complete market scene, continuous
clutter, tourist souvenir market, merchant caricature, camel market as default,
NPC crowd, festival bazaar, palace courtyard, readable signs, or catalogue-like
object pile.

**Applicable asset categories:** Environmental props, modular micro-clusters,
frontages, architectural attachments, selected safe edge overlays, and
practical environmental lighting.

**Treatment strength:** Strong for commerce props, clusters, and compatible
frontages; absent from unrelated gameplay categories unless a restrained city
accent is explicitly approved.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve object arrangement, density, and silhouette.
Night may reveal small practical light sources but cannot add festival lighting,
increase clutter, or merge assets into a scene.

**Optional atmosphere implications:** Dusk, mild dry-air haze, or a restrained
wind response in cloth may be separate variants. They remain removable and
must not change the cluster's role or procedural footprint.

One micro-cluster communicates one environmental idea. Human presence is
implied through objects, never through baked NPC crowds.

### Jaisalmer Pillar 4 — Restrained Craft and Material Detail

**Pillar strength:** Supporting-strength. This pillar supports the three
primary-strength pillars and must not dominate Jaisalmer identity.

**Identity statement:** Selective material and craft references add local
specificity through broad, simplified, removable details while preserving
silhouette clarity, moderate detail, and the shared flat-cartoon grammar.

**Supporting materials:** Sandstone, brass, clay, muted fabric, woven material,
wood, restrained painted surfaces, rope, and occasional iron fixtures.

**Silhouette and structural characteristics:** Broad geometric borders,
restrained carved edges, simplified lattice-inspired openings, rounded brass and
clay vessel forms, controlled painted panels, and hand-finished variation that
does not create fragile outlines.

**Relevant prop families:** Brass and clay vessels, restrained textile panels,
lattice-inspired opening inserts, simple border components, woven baskets,
hand-finished storage objects, small painted details, and modular carved-edge
accents.

**Surface treatments:** Limited broad carving, simplified weave, restrained
paint, quiet hand-finished variation, controlled brass highlights, and minimal
stone-edge detail. Ornament never covers an entire asset family.

**Lighting and atmosphere cues:** Daylight keeps material separation controlled
and readable. Night may provide small local highlights from approved practical
sources; craft detail cannot depend on glow, brightness, or reflective spectacle.

**Restrained palette contribution:** Scarce indigo, muted maroon, limited teal or
green, clay brown, controlled brass, and muted textile accents against sandstone
neutrals.

**Approved abstract motifs:** Simplified geometric borders, broad woven bands,
lattice tendencies, restrained carved-stone rhythm, vessel-ring geometry, and
small controlled painted accents.

**Prohibited stereotypes or misuse:** No decorative overload, ornament on every
prop or frontage, bright Rajasthan-festival rainbow, souvenir-shop craft wall,
excessive puppets, jewellery-market cliché, uncontrolled textile patterns, or
fragile carving-heavy silhouette.

**Applicable asset categories:** Selected environmental props, modular
micro-clusters, frontages, architectural details, and occasional safe secondary
accents where the category permits them.

**Treatment strength:** Supporting-strength; restrained even on strongly
city-skinned environment and minimal or absent on gameplay-critical categories.

**Day/night implications:** Preserve motif scale, placement, geometry, and
material meaning. Night cannot enlarge, illuminate, or recolour craft detail to
make it more prominent.

**Optional atmosphere implications:** Dry wind or dusk variants may change only
approved fabric response or local contrast. Craft remains readable and
subordinate without decorative motion.

### Environmental Density & Composition

**Low-to-medium overall density with compact edge clusters and larger breathing gaps.**

* Protect the playable road centre.
* Place identity primarily along the left and right edges.
* Use compact clusters instead of continuous markets.
* Allow architecture and individual anchors to breathe.
* Use occasional strong environmental anchors without landmark dependence.
* Preserve asymmetry and procedural-placement compatibility.
* Maintain greater openness than Mumbai without becoming empty or lifeless.
* Avoid dense Mumbai-style layering, uninterrupted frontage clutter, and
  decorative objects on the playable road.

### Shape Language

**Stepped sandstone masses with softened rectangular forms and restrained arches.**

Preserve clean flat-cartoon style, rounded forms with selective sharp accents,
flat fills with at most one darker shade, moderate detail, controlled outlines,
readable silhouettes, high bird’s-eye camera compatibility, and mobile
gameplay-scale readability.

Approved secondary forms include layered rectangular masses, stepped platforms,
shallow projections, parapets, restrained arch-like openings, rounded vessels,
softened fabric, simplified carts, and storage forms.

Reject ornate palace silhouettes, fragile carving-heavy profiles, excessive
dune-like curves, hyper-realistic architecture, and generic square blocks with
no researched local character.

### Material & Palette Direction

Primary material family:

* warm sandstone
* weathered plaster
* wood
* clay
* brass
* muted fabric
* dry compacted road material or asphalt where required

Secondary materials may include painted metal, iron fixtures, rope, baskets,
woven materials, restrained glass, and occasional concrete repairs.

Sandstone dominates architecture but not every asset. Metal, clay, fabric, and
wood provide controlled separation; modern materials appear only where
contextually appropriate. All materials use simplified flat-cartoon treatment.

Jaisalmer's palette direction is:

**Warm sandstone neutrals with restrained jewel and textile accents.**

The production palette family may use warm sandstone, muted ochre, dusty beige,
sun-faded cream, clay brown, restrained indigo, muted maroon, limited teal or
green, and controlled brass accents. Exact colour values remain asset-brief
parameters reviewed against the global gameplay colour system; this kit does
not freeze unsupported hexadecimal values.

Reject uniform yellow or gold, bright Rajasthan-festival rainbows, colour-only
city identity, or changes to global danger, warning, health, rarity, ownership,
and interaction colours. Saturated accents remain scarce.

### Day, Night & Optional Atmosphere

Day and night use the same structural Jaisalmer city skin.

**Day:** Warm clear daylight, restrained high-sun character, controlled surface
brightness, readable contact shadows, protected road visibility, subtle
atmospheric dryness, and warm but not heavily yellow colour grading.

Reject permanent golden hour, extreme orange grading, harsh realistic contrast,
blown highlights, large road-obscuring shadows, and cinematic desert spectacle.

**Night:** Restrained lanterns, warm practical bulbs, limited interior shop
light, subtle cool ambient separation, controlled edge lighting, restrained
local pools, and a quiet readable road centre.

Reject festival lighting everywhere, palace floodlighting, uncontrolled bloom,
excessive orange, large permanent glow pools, dark roads, and road-wide lighting
noise.

**Optional atmosphere variants:** Dry wind, mild windblown dust, subtle heat
haze, dusk lighting, restrained edge sand accumulation, dry-air haze, and
softened distant atmosphere. These are optional, bounded, removable variants,
not the permanent default.

Optional atmosphere must never obscure the player, enemies, attacks,
projectiles, hazards, pickups, road boundaries, or World-space UI. It cannot
replace gameplay communication or create continuous visual noise.

### Landmark & Iconic-reference Rules

**Landmark echoes rather than literal landmarks.**

Approved echoes may borrow sandstone construction logic, broad proportion,
stepped massing, parapet rhythm, projection patterns, restrained lattice
tendencies, simplified opening geometry, material, and structural rhythm.

Do not create Jaisalmer Fort replicas, fort-shaped props, palace replicas,
identifiable haveli reproductions, monument miniatures, tourist-view
compositions, postcard skylines, major fort backdrops, large desert-dune scenes,
or heritage-hotel compositions.

### Jaisalmer-specific Prohibitions

Reject:

* tourist-poster Jaisalmer and cinematic desert fantasy
* literal fort replicas, fort-shaped props, and palace miniatures
* excessive haveli ornamentation or identifiable haveli reproductions
* camels everywhere, camel-tour scenes, or camel markets as default identity
* endless dunes, permanent sandstorms, or desert tents everywhere
* royal processions, kings, queens, or palace characters
* generic Rajasthan contamination
* excessive puppets, jewellery-market clichés, and festival decoration everywhere
* uncontrolled textile patterns and bright rainbow palettes
* every surface appearing golden
* poverty caricatures
* decorative religious or political symbols
* readable Hindi or English text
* logos, trademarks, brand marks, and recognisable packaging
* Mumbai transport cues, Mumbai monsoon cues, or black-and-yellow transport striping
* unrelated foreign-desert materials, motifs, architecture, and cultural cues
* complete painted scenes and decorative clutter on the playable road

Abstract sign shapes and painted blocks contain no readable letters, words,
numbers, logos, or pseudo-text.

A camel may later exist only as a justified modular edge asset. It cannot become
the defining cue for Jaisalmer compositions or create a complete camel-tour or
market scene.

### Asset-category Treatment Strength

#### Strong Jaisalmer Treatment

* environmental props
* modular micro-clusters
* frontages and architectural elements
* road materials and safe road overlays
* practical environmental lighting

#### Moderate Jaisalmer Treatment

* approved hazards
* selected enemies and selected pickups
* restrained ambient VFX
* atmospheric overlays

#### Restrained Jaisalmer Treatment

* player
* attacks and projectiles
* functional VFX
* World-space UI and HUD

#### Minimal or No Jaisalmer Treatment

* critical telegraphs
* danger boundaries and ownership grammar
* globally fixed gameplay symbols
* accessibility-essential UI and cues

Jaisalmer treatment must never change gameplay meaning, category recognition,
silhouette logic, functional footprint, scale hierarchy, perceived hitbox,
ownership, danger or rarity meaning, direction, timing, movement language,
gameplay state, anchors, interface meaning, or accessibility-essential
communication.

### Shared & Jaisalmer-exclusive Assets

Prefer shared asset families and use:

```text
Base Asset → Shared Variant → Jaisalmer Material/Detail Layer → Time-of-Day Treatment → Runtime Instance
```

A Jaisalmer-exclusive asset is allowed only when it:

* directly supports one of the four approved Jaisalmer pillars
* cannot be represented credibly through a shared asset family
* remains modular and procedurally reusable
* follows the global style and shape language
* remains readable at mobile gameplay scale
* preserves category and protected gameplay characteristics
* does not create a complete scene or baked background
* has explicit written production justification

Novelty, ornament, charm, or beauty alone is not sufficient.

### Technical Production Rules

Every Jaisalmer asset brief inherits the relevant Prompt Bible category module,
City-skin Framework, Technical Asset Contract, naming and metadata conventions,
accessibility requirements, and in-game approval gate.

City treatment cannot change contract-governed dimensions, alpha behaviour,
transparent-background requirements, padding, pivots, anchors, scale,
perspective, orientation, frame consistency, slicing, naming, metadata, export,
safe-area behaviour, or supported runtime transformations unless an explicitly
approved technical variant requires it.

Assets remain isolated, modular, palette-compatible, and transparent-background
where their category requires it. Do not create complete streets, markets,
backgrounds, gameplay scenes, painted day/night pairs, flattened UI, or
unsupported animation and rendering behaviour.

### Jaisalmer Acceptance Criteria

Accept a Jaisalmer city-skinned output only when it:

1. Clearly derives from at least one applicable approved Jaisalmer pillar.
2. Reads as everyday inhabited desert-city Jaisalmer through multiple restrained cues.
3. Preserves the global Masala Run shape, style, camera, and gameplay hierarchy.
4. Preserves category, silhouette, footprint, ownership, direction, timing, and state.
5. Preserves accessibility and gameplay meaning without colour-only recognition.
6. Uses category-appropriate treatment strength.
7. Maintains low-to-medium density, compact edge clusters, and larger breathing gaps.
8. Protects the road centre and remains readable at mobile gameplay scale.
9. Preserves the same structural asset across day and night.
10. Keeps sand, dust, heat haze, dry wind, and dusk treatments optional and bounded.
11. Avoids literal landmarks, tourist stereotypes, generic Rajasthan, readable text, and mixed-city cues.
12. Remains modular, procedurally reusable, and technically contract-compliant.
13. Remains valid when optional Jaisalmer accents and atmosphere are reduced or removed.
14. Passes in-game review at target scale beside the courier.

### Frozen Jaisalmer Identity Summary

1. Jaisalmer means everyday inhabited desert-city Jaisalmer.
2. Its four pillars are Layered Sandstone Architecture, Desert-Climate
   Adaptation, Compact Desert Commerce and Courtyard Life, and
   supporting-strength Restrained Craft and Material Detail.
3. Density is low-to-medium with compact edge clusters and larger breathing gaps.
4. Shape rhythm uses stepped sandstone masses with softened rectangular forms.
5. The material family is sandstone-led but mixed with plaster, wood, clay,
   brass, muted fabric, and appropriate road material.
6. The palette uses warm sandstone neutrals with restrained jewel and textile accents.
7. Day uses warm clear light with restrained high-sun character.
8. Night uses restrained lantern and shop light with a quiet road centre.
9. Dry wind, dust, heat haze, dusk, and edge sand are optional variants.
10. Landmark echoes replace literal landmarks.
11. Tourist, royal, camel-led, and generic-Rajasthan stereotypes are prohibited.
12. Gameplay meaning, accessibility, and global readability remain protected.

This Jaisalmer identity is frozen. Changes require formal city-kit review,
documented justification, version/status updates, and impact review against the
Art Bible, Prompt Bible, Technical Asset Contract, and dependent asset briefs.
